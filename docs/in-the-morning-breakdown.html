01The Track — Review & Cultural Context

"In The Morning (Let Your Love Come In)" dropped in 2009 right at the peak of UK Funky's pirate-radio moment. Fuzzy Logik (also stylised Fuzzy Logic) handled the production; Egypt — a London-raised vocalist with a strong American soul lean — sang the hook. The record bubbled through Rinse FM, Choice FM and the FWD>>/East London club circuit before Virgin Records signed it for a wider release.

If UK Garage in the late 90s was champagne and a tight 2-step, UK Funky was rum punch, Soca whistles, Afrobeat shakers and a more open, four-on-the-floor (or syncopated) kick. "In The Morning" sits squarely in the genre's commercial sweet spot: a soulful 80s-leaning vocal floats over hard, stuttered drums and the digital-rubberised steel-pan stab that became one of the defining UK Funky sound signatures.

Wikipedia lists it alongside Crazy Cousinz "Do You Mind?", "Bongo Jam" and "Funky Anthem" as one of the genre's defining hits. Lil Silva — by then already a celebrated funky producer — turned in a sparser, more percussive remix the same year that's still rinsed in retro sets.

What makes it tick

  • The hook. "Let your love come in" — short, sing-along, melismatic, drops on the down-beat. Classic radio earworm engineering.
  • The stab. A bright, plucky steel-pan / FM-bell hybrid riff that answers the vocal phrase. It's the percussive lead and the melodic hook in one.
  • The drums. A "hard stutter" pattern (per The Fader's 2009 review) — think syncopated kicks plus tight tribal congas, shaker on the offbeats and a clap on 2/4 with extra ghost hits.
  • The space. Unusually for 2009 club music, the arrangement breathes. There are big drop-outs where only the kick + vocal sit. That's how it gets crowds singing.
  • The swing. Not full UKG triplet swing — more like 56–58% on 16ths. Just enough to lope.

My take

"In The Morning" is one of those records that on paper looks too simple: a four-bar loop, three chords, a 16-bar vocal cycle. In a sweaty room at 4am though, that simplicity is the whole point. It's the percussion programming, the swing, and Egypt's confident, lived-in delivery doing the heavy lifting. Whatever you build below — keep it sparse and let the groove cook.

A note on accuracy. The exact MIDI/stems of the original were never made public. What follows below is a faithful style-template reconstructed from genre conventions, contemporary reviews of the track (FADER, Resident Advisor, Boiler Room) and well-known UK Funky production techniques (Crazy Cousinz, Roska, Lil Silva, Hard House Banton, Apple). Treat it as a starting framework you can shape until your version feels like the record.

Programming tips

  • Velocity layers. Hats: 70 / 95 / 110 in a rolling pattern. Without this they sound stiff.
  • Tiny timing nudges. Push the conga hi +5 to +12 ticks behind the grid (Logic = +5–10ms). Pulls the groove lazy without losing the kick's authority.
  • One-shot kick under sustained sub. The kick is the transient; let the bass synth carry the body. Side-chain everything sub-and-below to it.
  • Conga = the song's identity. If you change one element, change the conga pattern — that's where the dancehall/Soca DNA lives.
  • Don't double-hit. UK Funky lives on space. If a 16th step could be empty, leave it empty.

Suggested Logic kit

Drum Machine Designer + a "House" or "Hip-Hop Modern" kit as the chassis, then swap in: a 909 kick from EXS24 → Vintage Drum Machines, a cowbell from World → Latin Percussion, and any conga sample from a Splice "UK Funky" pack. Add Drum Synth on its own bus for one extra clap layer.

Anatomy of the bass part

  • Note choice. Almost entirely roots and 5ths, with the occasional 7th passing tone before a chord change.
  • Rhythm. Hits on the "and" of 1, "and" of 2, the "e" of 3 and the "and" of 4. Whatever you do, do not double the kick — that kills the genre's stutter.
  • Slides. Short portamento (~25–45ms) on every other note; this is what makes Mariana sing.
  • Sub vs. mid-bass. Two layers are common: a clean sine sub (Mariana Layer 1) and a saturated mid-bass (Mariana Layer 2 with drive + filter resonance).

Side-chain behaviour

  • Side-chain the bass off the kick only, not the whole drum bus.
  • Logic Pro 12 ➜ Compressor on the bass channel, mode = Studio FET, side-chain key input = Drums > Kick.
  • Settings: Ratio 4:1 · Threshold to taste (target ~6 dB GR) · Attack 0.5–1 ms · Release 80–120 ms · Knee 12 dB.
  • Use the Compressor output meter to confirm the bass breathes rather than ducks awkwardly. The pump should feel musical.

The "answer" stab riff

The bright steel-pan / bell stab plays a 2-bar call-and-response motif over the chords. Approximate notes (over Am):

A5 — A5 — E5 — G5 · — · F5 — E5 (rest, rest, rest)

Then transposes along the chord progression — drop a 4th over F, up a tone over G, back to root over Em.

In Logic, route this stab patch to its own track with a tape delay (1/8 dotted, 35% feedback) and a subtle plate reverb. Pan it 15% right against the vocal which should sit dead centre.

Pad layer (optional)

Underneath the stab, a slow-attack analogue-style pad plays sustained 4-note voicings of the same chords. It's mostly felt, not heard — sub-bass to ~600 Hz only, with an automated low-pass that opens slightly into the choruses.

In Serum 2 use a 3-osc warm saw stack with one octave-down sine, slow filter envelope (~600 ms attack), and a wide stereo chorus.

DJ-friendly principles

  • Loop-able 8s and 16s. Every section length is a power of 8 bars so DJs can loop or cut on any boundary.
  • Long intro/outro. 16+ bars of mostly drums + sub at each end. Non-negotiable for club records.
  • Hold back the lead. Stab riff doesn't enter at full velocity until bar 25. Anticipation > everything.
  • Vocal chops in the drop. Single-syllable vocal stabs ("love", "in") replace full lines so the dance moment doesn't fight the hook.
  • Reverse cymbal + sub drop. One bar before each chorus. Two-bar tape stop into the final outro is a classic UK Funky move.

Logic Pro 12 — how to apply it

  1. Select the drum region(s).
  2. In the Region inspector ▸ Quantize set to 1/16 Swing E (E = ~58%) for the hat-leading group.
  3. For sub-percussion, use a custom groove template: import a UK Funky reference loop into a track, right-click ▸ Make Groove Template. Then quantize your perc to that template — instant authentic feel.
  4. Use the Q-Strength slider at ~85% to keep some humanising looseness.
  5. Apply Q-Velocity at 30–40% — pulls velocities toward the reference's dynamics without flattening them.

Pro tip: bounce a 1-bar loop of just kick + hats and listen on headphones with eyes closed. If your head nods before the kick lands, the swing is too aggressive. If it nods on the kick, you're on the money.

09Moog Mariana — Bassline Programming

Mariana is purpose-built for low-end and is ideal for UK Funky bass. The instrument has two layers (A & B); we'll use them as sub and mid-bass respectively, blended for a single thick voice that fills the bottom without smearing the kick.

Layer A — Clean Sine Sub

OSC 1Sine wave · Octave 16′ · Level 100%
OSC 2Off (or sine at −12 semitones, level 30%)
FilterLadder LP · Cutoff 100% · Res 0% (let it through clean)
Amp EnvA 1 ms · D 400 ms · S 100% · R 80 ms
Glide30–40 ms (legato mode ON)
Drive0% — keep it pure
HPF30 Hz on the layer output

Layer B — Saturated Mid-Bass

OSC 1Saw · Octave 8′ · Level 100%
OSC 2Square · −7 semitones · Level 60% (gives the growl)
FilterLadder LP · Cutoff 35% · Res 25% · Velocity to cutoff +30%
Amp EnvA 2 ms · D 250 ms · S 70% · R 120 ms
Filter EnvA 0 · D 180 ms · S 0% · R 100 ms · Amount +45%
Drive20–30% (analog drive — Mariana's signature)
FXBuilt-in chorus, light. Built-in delay OFF.

Macro & performance setup

  • Layer balance. Layer A around −2 dB, Layer B around −6 dB, then mix to taste once in the project. The sub should never louder than the mid-bass on a phone speaker — but it should be obvious on a sub.
  • Mod wheel ➜ Layer B filter cutoff. Use it live during the chorus to add bite.
  • Aftertouch ➜ Layer A volume +2 dB. Lean into long notes for emphasis.
  • Velocity ➜ Layer B filter (already set). Hard hits get more growl.
  • Mono / Legato. Always mono. Always legato. The slides are the whole point.
Don't stack delay on the bass. Mariana has lovely built-in delay but on this style it makes the bass smear. Keep delay strictly on the stab and the vocal. Bass = dry + side-chain.

10Rhodes Piano — The Optional Soul Layer

The original record doesn't lead with Rhodes, but adding a quiet Rhodes layer is the single biggest "soulful" upgrade you can make to a UK Funky-style production. Two ways to do it:

Option A — Logic stock (free)

  • Track ➜ Vintage Electric Piano ➜ preset Mark I Hard Mono.
  • Set Tine Volume 65% · Bell Volume 30% · Decay Time 50%.
  • Add Pedal emulation: ON, depth 25%.
  • Plug-ins: Chorus (rate 0.6 Hz) → Tape Delay (1/4 dotted, 18% mix, 30% feedback) → Channel EQ (HPF 200 Hz, gentle 5 kHz +1.5 dB).

Option B — Serum 2 fake-Rhodes

  • OSC A: Bell > Bell 2 · Unison 1.
  • OSC B: Basic > Triangle · same pitch · Level −6 dB.
  • FM A→B at 22% (this is the "tine" character).
  • Filter: SVF Low 24 · Cutoff 3.5 kHz · key-track 80%.
  • Amp env: A 1 ms · D 1.2 s · S 0% · R 250 ms.
  • FX: Chorus (light) → Tape Delay → Reverb (Spring, 1.6 s).

Rhodes voicings

Don't play the chord blocks. Play upper-structure thirds over the bass:

  • Over Am ➜ play C–E–G (top three of Am7) syncopated 16ths.
  • Over F ➜ play A–C–E.
  • Over G ➜ play B–D–F# (only chord with the F#, gives motion).
  • Over Em ➜ play G–B–D.

Pan 25% left, sit at −18 dB in the mix. Mute it during the drop and let it back in for the verses.

11Logic Pro 12 — Project Setup

Project & tempo

  • New empty project. Sample rate 48 kHz, bit depth 24-bit.
  • Tempo: 130 BPM. Time signature 4/4.
  • Project Settings ▸ Recording ▸ Count-in 1 bar.
  • Project Settings ▸ Audio ▸ Buffer 128 (tracking) / 512 (mixing).
  • Enable Flex Time only on the vocal track when needed; leave drums/bass un-flexed.

Track template

  1. 1 Drum bus (aux). Children: Kick, Clap, CH, OH, Conga Hi, Conga Lo, Shaker, Cowbell.
  2. 2 Bass bus (aux). Child: Mariana stereo out. Side-chain key from kick.
  3. 3 Synth bus (aux). Children: Stab (Serum 2), Pad (Serum 2), Vocal-doubler (Serum 2).
  4. 4 Keys bus. Children: Rhodes.
  5. 5 Vocal bus. Children: Lead vocal, BVs, ad-libs, FX vocals.
  6. 6 FX bus. Risers, sweeps, reverse cymbals.
  7. Master. Channel EQ → Linear Phase EQ → Bus Compressor → Adaptive Limiter.

Drum bus chain

  1. Channel EQ: HPF 30 Hz, gentle high-shelf +1.5 dB @ 12 kHz.
  2. Compressor (Studio FET): 4:1, attack 5 ms, release 60 ms, target 2 dB GR.
  3. Phat FX (or Bitcrusher at low intensity) for analog-feel grit — drive 10%, mix 12%.
  4. Send to Reverb aux (Space Designer, "Small Wood Room", −18 dB).

Vocal chain (in this order)

  1. Channel EQ: HPF 100 Hz, notch at 250 Hz (mud), shelf +2 dB @ 8 kHz (air).
  2. DeEsser 2: 7.5 kHz, range −6 dB.
  3. Compressor (Vintage VCA): 3:1, attack 8 ms, release 120 ms, target 4 dB GR.
  4. Compressor 2 (Studio FET): catch peaks, 6 dB GR ceiling.
  5. Channel EQ (post): subtle presence boost +1 dB @ 2.5 kHz.
  6. Sends: Tape Delay (1/4 dot, 25% mix), ChromaVerb "Plate" (1.4 s, 12% mix).

Smart Tempo / Smart Controls

Logic 12's Smart Tempo isn't really needed since you're starting from scratch at 130 BPM, but if you import a Crazy Cousinz or Roska reference for groove-template purposes, set Project Tempo to Adapt while you analyse, then revert to Keep before tracking.

Map a single Smart Control macro to: Filter Cutoff (Stab) + Reverb Send (Vocal) + Drum Bus Volume. Automate it for the breaks — one fader, three musical results.

Master bus chain (in order)

  1. Channel EQ (clean) — HPF 25 Hz, notches as needed.
  2. Linear Phase EQ — broad tonal sculpt (±1.5 dB max).
  3. Bus Compressor — Ratio 2:1 · Attack 30 ms · Release Auto · target 1.5 dB GR · mix 80% (parallel).
  4. Multipressor — three-band, gentle, control sub specifically.
  5. Stereo Spread — only above 800 Hz, intensity 25%.
  6. Adaptive Limiter — out ceiling −1 dBTP, lookahead 5 ms.
  7. Loudness Meter (verify −8 LUFS-I for club, −14 for streaming).

FX & ear-candy moments

  • Reverse cymbal 1 bar before each chorus. Logic stock Vintage Cymbal Reverse.
  • Sub-drop (sine sweep 80→30 Hz over 1 bar) at every drop bar. Sidechained to the kick.
  • Vinyl noise at −38 dB across the whole track for warmth.
  • Tape stop 2 bars before the outro. Logic Pro 12's Vintage Tape Stop in Pedalboard.
  • Delay throws on the last word of each vocal phrase. Bus send automation, not insert.
Reference levels. Mix on a sub-equipped system if you have one. If not, A/B with Crazy Cousinz "Do You Mind?", Donae'o "Party Hard" or Hard House Banton "Sirens" — all roughly the same era and mastering style. Your kick should sit just under theirs by 0.5 dB so your record doesn't fight references on a club system.
