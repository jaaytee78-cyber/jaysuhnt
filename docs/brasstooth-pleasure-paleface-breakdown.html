01The Track — Review & Cultural Context

The original "Pleasure" by Brasstooth (London producer who'd been making records since 1992) featuring vocalist Frances James dropped in 1999 and is widely listed alongside Cookies & Cream and Pure Silk-era cuts as one of the great UK Garage records. Brasstooth himself lists it under the same DNA as Gas Club / Cookies & Cream / Exposure — that soulful, 2-step swung side of UKG that ran the Sunday-night raves before the genre splintered.

By the late 2000s the UKG underground had bent into UK Funky — same urban London ecosystem, same producers, same MCs, but at ~130 BPM with tribal/Soca percussion and the genre's signature sub-bass-on-the-offbeat. DJ Paleface sat right at the genesis of this scene: cousin of Flukes, founder of Northern Line Records, and one half of Crazy Cousinz — the duo whose 2008 "Do You Mind" remix for Kyla became one of the foundational UK Funky records (and was later sampled by Drake on "One Dance").

Paleface's flip of "Pleasure" is exactly what you'd expect a UK Funky producer of his pedigree to do with a UKG record he loved: keep Frances James' vocal hook intact (the song's identity), strip the original 2-step drums, lay down a 4-on-the-floor + tribal-perc bed at 130 BPM, drop in a Crazy-Cousinz-style creeping sub-bass, and let it run on dubplate in a sweaty room.

What the remix does (and why it works)

  • Vocal preserved. Frances James' soulful, sung lead is the through-line. Paleface chops the verse for the breaks but keeps the chorus intact.
  • Drums reborn. The original 2-step swung kick is replaced with a hard 4-on-the-floor kick + tribal congas + offbeat shaker. Same shuffle feel, very different drive.
  • Bassline redesigned. Paleface's signature is a "creeping" sub-bass that sits in the gaps the kick leaves — exactly the move that defines "Do You Mind" Crazy Cousinz Mix.
  • Chord stabs simplified. Where the original used jazzy garage 7th chords, the remix typically uses tighter 3-note voicings on a brighter Rhodes/synth so they punch through a club PA.
  • Arrangement tightened. Long club intro, vocal entry, drop, break, drop again — bounce-friendly and DJ-friendly.

My take

Bootleg/edit-style flips of soulful UKG records were one of the quiet engines of UK Funky's 2008–2010 peak. They re-introduced an older audience (who knew the original from raves) to the new sound, and they gave selectors a credible bridge between sets. Paleface's "Pleasure" sits in exactly that lineage — it's a love-letter from one London scene to another, and it works because Frances James' vocal is timeless. Build your version sparse, let the bass creep, and don't bury the voice.

A note on accuracy. The Paleface remix appears to be a producer-hosted edit/bootleg (Reverbnation, no official commercial pressing on Discogs). I've been unable to fetch the page's audio for transcription, so what follows is a faithful style-template reconstruction: the original Brasstooth/Frances James "Pleasure" DNA fused with Paleface's documented production signatures (Crazy Cousinz "Do You Mind" creeping bass, Northern Line UKF arrangement conventions, Pioneer/Supa D-era percussion programming). Treat it as a starting framework — shape it until your version sits next to the Reverbnation file.

Programming notes for "Paleface feel"

  • Kick steady, hats shuffled. The original 2-step lives on as the hat pattern. Don't be tempted to syncopate the kick.
  • Velocity layers. Hats: 65 / 90 / 110 in a rolling pattern, never uniform.
  • Conga = the soul of the track. Borrow exactly the conga placements from "Do You Mind" (Crazy Cousinz Mix): one on the "e" of 1, one on the "a" of 2, sparse on bar 4.
  • Tiny timing nudges. Push the conga hi +5–10ms behind grid. Push the shaker +3ms ahead of grid. Lazy congas + driving shaker = lope.
  • Side-chain everything. Drum bus is the trigger, sub-bass + pad + Rhodes all duck off it.
  • Don't double-hit. If a 16th step could be empty, leave it empty. Space is the genre.

04Bassline — The Paleface Creeping Sub

If the conga is the soul of the track, the bassline is its identity. Paleface's signature — used on "Do You Mind" Crazy Cousinz Mix and many Northern Line records — is a creeping sub-bass that moves in unpredictable offbeats and slides between root notes with short portamento. Notes sit in the gaps the kick leaves, never on the kick itself. Click a cell to cycle through F-minor scale notes.

Default pattern is the F-minor "creeping" bass: F → C → Db → Eb → F across the bar, locked to off-16ths. Cycle each cell to swap notes; toggle to REST for silence.

Anatomy of the creeping bass

  • Note choice. Roots, 5ths, and the b7 — almost no 3rds (keeps it ambiguous between minor and Dorian).
  • Rhythm. Hits on the "e" of 1, "and" of 2, "a" of 3, "and" of 4. Never on the kick.
  • Slides. 30–50ms portamento between every note. Mariana legato mode is non-negotiable.
  • Two layers. Sub sine (Mariana A) for weight + saturated saw (Mariana B) for definition. Mid-bass slightly louder on phone speakers, sub louder on subs.
  • Drop the bass for the chorus. Pull bass out for 2 bars at the start of each chorus. The drop back in is the dancefloor moment.

Side-chain behaviour (Logic Pro 12)

  • Side-chain bass off kick only, not the drum bus.
  • Compressor on bass channel · mode Studio FET · key input: Drums > Kick.
  • Ratio 4:1 · Threshold to taste (~6 dB GR target) · Attack 0.5 ms · Release 90 ms · Knee 12 dB.
  • The pump should feel musical — bass breathes between kick hits, doesn't punch out.

Vocal ducking (signature Paleface move)

Side-chain the bass also off the lead vocal at very low ratio (1.5:1, 2 dB GR max). When Frances James sings the long-held "pleasure" notes, the bass softens by ~2 dB. Subliminal but it's why his remixes feel so vocal-forward.

The "answer" stab riff

A bright synth/Rhodes stab plays a 2-bar call-and-response motif over the chords, answering Frances James' vocal phrase. Approximate notes (over Fm):

F5 — F5 — C5 — Eb5 · — · Db5 — C5 (rest, rest)

Then transposes along the progression — drop a 4th over Db, up a tone over Ab, back to root over Eb.

Pan the stab 15% right against the centred vocal. Send to a 1/8-dotted tape delay (35% feedback) and a small plate reverb.

Pad layer (optional but recommended)

Underneath the stab, a slow-attack analogue-style pad plays sustained 4-note voicings of the same chords. It sits at −20 dB — felt, not heard — and an automated low-pass opens slightly into each chorus.

In Serum 2: 3-osc warm saw stack with one sub-octave sine, slow filter envelope (~600 ms attack), wide stereo chorus. Roll off below 200 Hz and above 7 kHz.

DJ-friendly principles (Northern Line conventions)

  • Loop-able 8s and 16s. Every section is a multiple of 8 so DJs can cut on any boundary.
  • Long intro / outro. 16+ bars of mostly drums + sub at each end — non-negotiable for club records.
  • Hold back the lead. Stab riff doesn't enter at full velocity until bar 25. Anticipation everywhere.
  • Vocal chops in the drop. Single-syllable Frances James stabs ("plea-", "love") replace full lines so the dance moment doesn't fight the hook.
  • 2-bar tape stop into the final outro. Pure 2009 Pirate-radio energy.

Logic Pro 12 — how to apply it

  1. Select the drum region(s).
  2. In Region inspector ▸ Quantize set 1/16 Swing E for the hat-leading group.
  3. For sub-perc, build a custom groove template: import any Crazy Cousinz / Paleface reference loop, right-click ▸ Make Groove Template, then quantize your perc to it.
  4. Q-Strength ~85% — keep some humanisation.
  5. Q-Velocity 30–40% — pulls velocity toward the reference without flattening.

If your head nods before the kick lands, swing is too much. If it lands on the kick, you're on the money.

Mariana is purpose-built for low-end and is ideal for Paleface's signature creeping bass. Two layers (A & B) used as sub + mid-bass, blended into a single thick voice that fills the bottom without smearing the kick.

Macro & performance setup

  • Layer balance. Layer A −2 dB, Layer B −5 dB. Slightly more mid-bass than the In The Morning template — Paleface's bass needs to cut on phone speakers.
  • Mod wheel ➜ Layer B filter cutoff. Push it during the chorus for extra bite.
  • Aftertouch ➜ Layer A volume +2 dB on long sustained notes.
  • Velocity ➜ Layer B filter (already set). Hard hits = more growl.
  • Mono / Legato. Always mono. Always legato. The slides are everything.
Don't stack delay on the bass. Mariana has nice built-in delay but on this style it smears. Keep delay strictly on the stab and the vocal. Bass = dry + side-chain.

10Rhodes Piano — The Soul Layer

The original Brasstooth Pleasure had a Rhodes-leaning piano stab — that's part of why it became a soulful UKG anthem. The Paleface remix keeps that DNA but pushes it lower in the mix and shorter in note duration, so it acts as harmonic punctuation rather than a lead.

Option A — Logic stock (free)

  • Track ➜ Vintage Electric Piano ➜ preset Mark I Hard Mono.
  • Tine Volume 70% · Bell Volume 35% · Decay Time 45%.
  • Pedal emulation ON, depth 25%.
  • Plug-ins: Chorus (rate 0.6 Hz) → Tape Delay (1/4 dotted, 18% mix, 30% feedback) → Channel EQ (HPF 200 Hz, +1.5 dB @ 5 kHz).

Option B — Serum 2 fake-Rhodes

  • OSC A: Bell > Bell 2 · Unison 1.
  • OSC B: Basic > Triangle · same pitch · Level −6 dB.
  • FM A→B at 22% (the "tine" character).
  • Filter: SVF Low 24 · Cutoff 3.5 kHz · key-track 80%.
  • Amp env: A 1 ms · D 1.2 s · S 0% · R 250 ms.
  • FX: Chorus (light) → Tape Delay → Spring Reverb (1.6 s).

Rhodes voicings (over the F-minor cycle)

Don't play the chord blocks. Play upper-structure thirds over the bass:

  • Over Fm ➜ Ab–C–Eb (top three of Fm7) syncopated 16ths.
  • Over Db ➜ F–Ab–C.
  • Over Ab ➜ C–Eb–G (only chord with the major 7th, gives motion).
  • Over Eb ➜ G–Bb–D.

Pan 25% left, sit at −20 dB. Mute during the drop and let it back in for verses.

Frances James vocal chain (in this order)

  1. Channel EQ: HPF 100 Hz, notch at 250 Hz (mud), shelf +2 dB @ 8 kHz (air).
  2. DeEsser 2: 7.5 kHz, range −6 dB (Frances has bright sibilance).
  3. Compressor (Vintage VCA): 3:1, attack 8 ms, release 120 ms, target 4 dB GR.
  4. Compressor 2 (Studio FET): catch peaks, 6 dB GR ceiling.
  5. Channel EQ (post): subtle presence boost +1 dB @ 3 kHz.
  6. Sends: Tape Delay (1/4 dot, 25% mix), ChromaVerb "Plate" (1.4 s, 12% mix).
  7. Bus send to Vocal-doubler Serum patch at −18 dB for chorus thickness.
